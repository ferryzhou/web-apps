Arbitrage Strategy Lab

One idea, many markets: the same thing sells at different prices in different places — buy where it's cheap, sell where it's dear. Sports odds differences are one instance; so are cross-exchange crypto gaps, wholesale-vs-retail spreads, and dual-listed stocks. This lab is a thinking tool: a taxonomy of where price gaps live, a screening framework for whether a gap is actually money, an edge calculator, and a research-plan generator. Siblings: betting case studies · live spread monitor.

The core equation

Every unequal-price trade — betting dutch, crypto transfer, retail flip — is the same trade. It makes money only if the gap survives all costs and both legs actually execute:

net edge = (Psell − Pbuy) − feesbuy − feessell − transport/settlement − slippage − capital cost × time
EV per cycle = pboth legs fill × net edge − (1 − pfill) × one-leg loss

The second line is the one beginners skip. A "risk-free" 2% gap with a 5% loss when one leg fails and a 90% fill rate is EV = 0.9×2% − 0.1×5% = +1.3%; at a 70% fill rate it's −0.1% — negative. Leg risk, not spotting gaps, is where arbitrage actually gets decided. The second question beginners skip: why does this gap exist, and who is happy paying it? A gap with no explanation is usually a data error, a hidden cost, or a trap (stale quote, unfillable size, different asset than you think).

The screening framework — is this gap actually money?

Five tests, in order. Most "opportunities" die at 1 or 3.

TestQuestionClassic failure
1 · Same-claim testAre the two things exactly the same claim on the world — same settlement rules, same delivery, same counterparty quality?Bookmaker voids a bet the exchange settles ("palpable error"); 90-min market vs to-advance market; BTC on a solvent vs shaky exchange.
2 · Staleness testAre both prices from the same instant, and still available at executable size?One venue's quote is minutes old — the gap is a timestamp artifact (most screener "arbs" die here).
3 · All-in cost testDoes the gap survive every fee, spread, transfer, tax, and the capital parked idle?2% crypto gap minus 0.2%+0.2% trading, 0.1% withdrawal, and a 10-minute transfer that moves 1%.
4 · Capacity testHow much size before you move the price or hit limits? Edge% × capacity = actual dollars.A 5% edge that takes $50 max stake before the book limits your account is lunch money.
5 · Persistence testWhy does it exist? Who is the loser and why do they tolerate it? What's the barrier protecting it from being competed away?No answer = it's already gone, or you're the loser (adverse selection: fast traders let you "win" only stale quotes).
The barrier is the business. Durable gaps persist because of a moat someone can't or won't cross: regulatory walls (licensed books can't serve every state), KYC/geography (kimchi premium), inventory risk (retail returns), or relationship limits (bookmakers ban winners). Your plan must say which barrier you can live behind — that barrier, not the spread, is what you're really monetizing.

    Risk gallery — how "risk-free" trades lose money

    • Leg risk. You fill the cheap side; the dear side moves before you fill it. Now you hold a naked position you never wanted. In-play betting and fast crypto moves make this the dominant cost.
    • Settlement mismatch. The two venues resolve the "same" event differently: bet void rules, extra-time treatment (a 90-minute draw is a win on the 3-way line and a loss on "to advance"), token versions, delivery grades.
    • Counterparty risk. Your profit sits on the weaker venue by construction — arbitrage systematically accumulates balances exactly where withdrawal is least certain (exchange insolvencies, slow bookmaker withdrawals, frozen accounts pending KYC).
    • Relationship risk. Venues that lose to you fire you: bookmaker stake limits and account closures typically arrive within weeks of consistent winning; retail platforms suspend reseller accounts.
    • Inventory & time risk. Anything you must hold between legs (goods in transit, coins on a chain, tickets before the event) can reprice against you; the gap pays you partly for bearing this.
    • Data risk. The gap was never real: stale quote, wrong market matched, decimal/American confusion, fee tier you don't actually get. Verification before execution is a step, not a vibe — the same staleness filtering the spread monitor applies to live quotes.
