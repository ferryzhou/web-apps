Consolidated learnings

  • Solve your own company's pain with an agent first, then productize it. Start with one repetitive task, shadow your best person doing it, and put the agent in Slack with a human approval step. d0, V and the lead agent all began this way.
  • Give agents fewer, simpler tools. Bash plus good files beat a pile of custom tools for d0.
  • Sandbox every agent and plan for prompt injection. Agents chase a goal for a long time and read instructions broadly.
  • Manage agents like an engineering manager: ask them to explain, have agents try to break your systems, and check final output yourself. When output is wrong, fix the agent's skills and evals, not only the output.
  • Stay model-agnostic. Route by cost and speed, with frontier models planning and cheaper open models executing.
  • Measure how long a customer signal takes to become a shipped fix. He treats that as a grade for how agentic a company is.

Coding agents now trigger most Vercel deployments

  • moved a metric
  • 3% to 60% of deployments in 2026

Rauch says the share of Vercel deployments that come from coding agents rose from 3% at the start of 2026 to 60% by October.

Details
  • The figure counts deployments made by coding agents such as Claude Code and Codex on behalf of users.
  • In June he put it at more than half, and said teams using Claude Code deploy about five times more often.
  • Vercel added about 1.5 billion deployments in 10 months, after taking about 10 years to reach the first billion.
  • Vercel's response was to build sandboxes, an AI Gateway and agent-readable pages for this traffic.

d0 data agent replaces growth of the data team

  • solved a problem
  • About 30,000 questions answered in one month

Vercel's internal data analyst agent d0 answers plain-English business questions in Slack. Rauch says it handled about 30,000 questions in a month, so Vercel did not need to keep growing its data team.

Details
  • d0 turns questions into SQL against Vercel's data warehouse and semantic layer.
  • In late 2025 the team cut about 80% of its specialized tools and gave it bash and the raw semantic files, which Vercel says made it faster and more accurate.
  • It sends Rauch a Monday business analysis of requests, compute and token trends.
  • Humans still decide what to do with the answers.

Agent pipeline handles AI SDK issues and pull requests

  • moved a metric
  • Up to 35% of merged PRs, 25% fewer open bugs

Vercel built an open-source, human-supervised factory of agents for the AI SDK repo. Four weeks in, Vercel reported it authored up to 35% of merged pull requests and cut open bugs by about 25%.

Details
  • Separate agents triage issues, write code, test and fix bugs.
  • Work covers bug fixes, docs updates and backports.
  • Nothing merges without approval from a human on the AI SDK team.
  • Vercel reported it closed most of July's issues (70% in one report, over 75% in another).

Source: Vercel blog, Building a software factory for AI SDK, Aug 2026 · Aug 12, 2026

Lead agent lets one SDR do the work of ten

  • moved a metric
  • Inbound SDR team of 10 down to 1

Vercel shadowed its best SDR and built an agent that researches and qualifies inbound leads and drafts replies. Vercel says one person now handles the work that took 10, and the other nine moved to more complex sales work.

Details
  • A GTM engineer watched the top SDR's process, including every tab she opened.
  • The agent runs deep research, qualifies the lead and drafts an email.
  • A human approves each lead and draft in Slack before anything is sent.
  • Vercel COO Jeanne DeWitt Grosser later said the whole agent costs about $5,000 a year to run.

Interview notes

Guillermo Rauch: Building the Internet for Agents

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch with Molly O'Shea on Sourcery (Oct 2026). At the start of 2026, 3% of Vercel deployments came from coding agents. By October it was 60%. "The machines have taken over."

How Vercel uses agents

  • "Almost everything we do at Vercel, we do with agents." Sales reps ask a Slack agent about an account instead of opening Salesforce, and the answer is tailored to their role.
  • An agent rewrote the first version of a Formula 1 team's migration in five minutes. That used to take months.
  • When an agent visits vercel.com, it gets precise text about the business instead of the visual site. Agents now buy services from Vercel's marketplace with no human involved.

His practices

  • Act like an engineering manager toward agents. Read the code, have the AI explain what it did, write tests, poke holes.
  • Don't lock into one model. About 80% of Vercel's AI token traffic now goes to cheaper open models. A common pattern: a frontier model writes the plan and an open model carries it out.
  • He doesn't watch videos. He pulls the transcript, skims it, then asks his agent questions.

Lessons

  • Agents are "soldiers with no frontal lobe." They will do anything to finish the mission. Sandbox them and plan for prompt injection.
  • Treat code as a liability. Critical systems need stricter rules than prototypes.
  • Judge by retention, not hype. Users have little loyalty to any one model.
  • Hire for the ability to direct many agents, not for having "vibe-coded this thing."
