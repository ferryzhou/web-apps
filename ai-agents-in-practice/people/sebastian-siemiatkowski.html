Sebastian Siemiatkowski is the co-founder and CEO of Klarna, the Swedish payments and banking company. In 2026 he described an in-house AI support agent doing the work of 600 to 850 people, a cut in SaaS vendors to give AI full context, and a partial return to human support after over-optimizing for cost.

Agent stories

AI assistant handles two-thirds of support chats

  • moved a metric
  • 2.3M chats in month one; work of 700 agents

Klarna launched an OpenAI-powered support assistant in February 2024 and published first-month results. He keeps citing it in 2026, now at about 850 agents' worth of work.

Details
  • First four weeks: 2.3 million conversations, two-thirds of all support chats, in 35 languages.
  • Klarna said CSAT was on par with humans, repeat inquiries fell 25%, and resolution time fell from 11 minutes to under 2.
  • It projected a $40M profit improvement for 2024.
  • Work came from outsourced agents; he says no Klarna employees were laid off.
  • In 2026 he gives 600 (20VC), 700 and 850 (Charter, Semafor, Goldman) as the agent-equivalent figure.

Source: Klarna announcement, Feb 2024 (via tech.eu); updated at Charter summit, Feb 2026 · Feb 2024

Klarna brings humans back as VIP support

  • solved a problem
  • Customer-agent satisfaction 'through the roof' (no figure)

After prioritizing cost, Klarna began hiring human agents again in May 2025 and now positions human support as a premium option. In 2026 it recruits passionate customers to work support shifts, Uber-style.

Details
  • He says Klarna historically put cost over quality and over-optimized support for it.
  • AI handles simple questions such as whether a payment went through.
  • Klarna hired a different type of agent, paid them more and gave them better tools.
  • Customers can reject the bot and ask for a human.
  • He blames past failures mainly on AI not being hooked up to source systems with full context.

Source: 20VC, Feb 2026, ~10:31; Compound Interest, Jun 2026 · Feb 2026

Cutting SaaS vendors to give AI full context

  • solved a problem

Klarna started closing down SaaS tools about two years before Feb 2026 so company data is less fragmented and easier for AI to use. Its support AI is built in-house because some answers need the source code.

Details
  • Example: explaining how interest is calculated can require reading code, since docs may be wrong.
  • He says they cannot buy support AI off the shelf because it becomes part of the tech stack.
  • Some SaaS such as Slack stays.
  • He tested a 'company in a box': open-source accounting and CRM with a Claude agent on top doing bookkeeping.

Source: 20VC, Feb 2026, ~10:31; Compound Interest, Jun 2026 · Feb 2026

CEO builds a pricing dashboard in 40 minutes

  • built a feature
  • ~40 minutes

While a team presented a pricing strategy from a Google Sheet, he dropped the sheet into Cursor with a prompt and built a visualization and dashboard. He says it took about 40 minutes.

Details

Revenue per employee more than triples

  • moved a metric
  • ~$400K to ~$1.4M per employee

Klarna shrank from about 6,000 to 7,000 people to under 3,000 mostly through attrition while revenue roughly doubled. He says AI helped, but some gains came from simply focusing on efficiency.

Details
  • Attrition runs about 15 to 20% a year and hiring stays below it; no major layoffs, he says.
  • Average pay per employee rose almost 50%, framed as sharing the gains.
  • He expects headcount below 2,000 by 2030.
  • Figures differ by talk: $300K to $1.3M (Feb) and $400K to $1.4M (Sep).

Source: Goldman Sachs Communacopia, Sep 2026; 20VC, Feb 2026, ~33:59 · Sep 2026

Klarna Shopping Search app launches in ChatGPT

  • launched something
  • 100M+ products, 13 markets

Klarna launched a Shopping Search app inside ChatGPT on May 20, 2026, built on its Product Search MCP server. Users get live prices, availability and offers across merchants.

Details
  • Covers over 100 million products and 400 million merchant listings in 13 markets, per Klarna.
  • Users are sent to the merchant's site to buy.
  • He argues brand preference still decides which payment method an agent uses.
  • At Goldman he called Klarna's catalog one of the largest after Google Shopping.

Source: Klarna press release, May 2026; Compound Interest, Jun 2026 · May 20, 2026

Key learnings

  • Give AI the company's full context: consolidate data off fragmented SaaS and let it read policies and even source code.
  • Build in-house when the agent's answers depend on your own systems; buy when they don't.
  • Start the agent on simple, high-volume questions and keep an easy path to a human.
  • Do not optimize support for cost alone; measure quality and treat human help as a premium service.
  • Shrink through slower hiring and attrition rather than layoffs, and share gains through higher pay.
  • Leaders should build with AI tools themselves to understand what is changing.
  • View change: in 2024 the story was AI replacing 700 agents; by 2026 he says Klarna over-optimized for cost, rehired humans for VIP support, and blames failures on poor integration, not AI quality.

Interview notes

Sebastian Siemiatkowski: Klarna's AI Bet, and What It Fixed

Klarna CEO Sebastian Siemiatkowski with Harry Stebbings on 20VC (Feb 2026). Klarna went from about 7,000 people to under 3,000, mostly by not backfilling departures. Its AI customer service does the work of about 600 agents.

What Klarna did

  • Built customer service AI in-house. Simple payment questions came first. Harder ones, like how interest is calculated, needed access to the source code, which is why they built instead of bought.
  • Moved data off standard SaaS tools so the AI has the whole company's context. "We need to provide our AI the best context."
  • Shrank mainly through roughly 20% yearly attrition and few new hires. Pay per person is up about 50%.

What they corrected

  • They over-optimized support for cost. Now they put more weight on human support, recruiting passionate customers as part-time support staff.
  • The cost-cutting story hurt their reputation.

Lessons

  • Context is the main input. Off-the-shelf AI can't see your codebase or your policies.
  • "The future of VIP experience will be the human connection."
  • He still codes himself with Claude and Cursor.
  • Headcount figures are his own. The transcript gives both 6,000 and 7,000 as the starting point.

All headcount, agent-equivalent and revenue-per-employee figures are Klarna's or his own claims, and they vary between talks (600, 700 or 850 agents; 6,000 or 7,000 starting staff). Only the 20VC interview has a verified YouTube upload; the Charter, Semafor and Goldman sources are articles and transcripts, and the Semafor summary was not checked against audio.
