Sebastian Siemiatkowski

Sebastian Siemiatkowski is the co-founder and CEO of Klarna, the Swedish payments and banking company. In 2026 he described an in-house AI support agent doing the work of 600 to 850 people, a cut in SaaS vendors to give AI full context, and a partial return to human support after over-optimizing for cost.

Latest view (Sep 9, 2026): As of September 2026 he credits AI with part, not all, of Klarna's rise to about $1.4M revenue per employee, and keeps human support as a premium option next to an AI assistant doing about 850 agents' work.

Consolidated learnings

  • Give AI full context: consolidate data off fragmented SaaS so agents can read policies and even source code.
  • Build in-house when the agent's answers depend on your own systems; buy when they don't.
  • Start the agent on simple, high-volume questions and keep an easy path to a human.
  • When the agent misses, check its hookup to source systems before blaming the model.
  • Do not optimize support for cost alone; treat human help as a premium service and pay for better agents.
  • Shrink through attrition and slower hiring rather than layoffs, and share gains through higher pay.
  • Leaders should build with AI tools themselves to understand what is changing.

AI assistant handles two-thirds of support chats

  • moved a metric
  • 2.3M chats in month one; work of 700 agents

Klarna launched an OpenAI-powered support assistant in February 2024 and published first-month results. He keeps citing it in 2026, now at about 850 agents' worth of work.

Details
  • First four weeks: 2.3 million conversations, two-thirds of all support chats, in 35 languages.
  • Klarna said CSAT was on par with humans, repeat inquiries fell 25%, and resolution time fell from 11 minutes to under 2.
  • It projected a $40M profit improvement for 2024.
  • Work came from outsourced agents; he says no Klarna employees were laid off.
  • In 2026 he gives 600 (20VC), 700 and 850 (Charter, Semafor, Goldman) as the agent-equivalent figure.

Source: Klarna announcement, Feb 2024 (via tech.eu); updated at Charter summit, Feb 2026 · Feb 2024

Cutting SaaS vendors to give AI full context

  • solved a problem

Klarna started closing down SaaS tools about two years before Feb 2026 so company data is less fragmented and easier for AI to use. Its support AI is built in-house because some answers need the source code.

Details
  • Example: explaining how interest is calculated can require reading code, since docs may be wrong.
  • He says they cannot buy support AI off the shelf because it becomes part of the tech stack.
  • Some SaaS such as Slack stays.
  • He tested a 'company in a box': open-source accounting and CRM with a Claude agent on top doing bookkeeping.

Interview notes

Sebastian Siemiatkowski: Klarna's AI Bet, and What It Fixed

Klarna CEO Sebastian Siemiatkowski with Harry Stebbings on 20VC (Feb 2026). Klarna went from about 7,000 people to under 3,000, mostly by not backfilling departures. Its AI customer service does the work of about 600 agents.

What Klarna did

  • Built customer service AI in-house. Simple payment questions came first. Harder ones, like how interest is calculated, needed access to the source code, which is why they built instead of bought.
  • Moved data off standard SaaS tools so the AI has the whole company's context. "We need to provide our AI the best context."
  • Shrank mainly through roughly 20% yearly attrition and few new hires. Pay per person is up about 50%.

What they corrected

  • They over-optimized support for cost. Now they put more weight on human support, recruiting passionate customers as part-time support staff.
  • The cost-cutting story hurt their reputation.

Lessons

  • Context is the main input. Off-the-shelf AI can't see your codebase or your policies.
  • "The future of VIP experience will be the human connection."
  • He still codes himself with Claude and Cursor.
  • Headcount figures are his own. The transcript gives both 6,000 and 7,000 as the starting point.
