Consolidated learnings

  • Automate small, repeatable experiments first (copy, minor UI) and score each stage of the loop with evals before widening scope.
  • Keep a human approving experiments and owning cross-functional alignment.
  • Re-test tasks after each model release. CASH did not work before Opus 4.5 and started working with Opus 4.6.
  • Put recurring checks on a schedule (dashboards, Slack misalignment, weekly feedback, admin) and track their false positives and false negatives.
  • Let engineers act as PM on projects of two engineer-weeks or less, and prototype instead of writing PRDs when you can.
  • Spend most effort on big bets; the product will be much better soon, so small tweaks matter less.
  • Treat activation as the main growth problem. Ask users who they are and route them, even if it adds friction.

Agent stories

CASH: Claude runs growth experiments

  • moved a metric
  • Win rate about that of a junior PM (2 to 3 years in)

The growth platform team built CASH (Claude Accelerates Sustainable Hypergrowth) to automate growth experimentation. It ships copy changes and small UI tweaks, and Avasare says it is delivering results.

Details
  • Started a couple of months before April 2026. He says it did not work before Opus 4.5 and started heading in the right direction with Opus 4.6.
  • The loop has four stages: find opportunities, build the change, check it against the quality and brand bar, analyze results. Each stage is scored with evals.
  • Scope so far is copy changes and very minor UI tweaks.
  • A human approves experiments. Cross-functional stakeholder work stays with people.
  • He puts the win rate at a junior PM level, below a senior PM.

Weekly Cowork scan finds team misalignment in Slack

  • solved a problem
  • Several major misalignments caught on the enterprise team

Avasare has Cowork scan Slack every week, using the Slack MCP, for places where teams are misaligned. Scott, who leads the enterprise team, used the same approach and found major misalignments that would have caused overlapping work.

Details
  • Setup: a scheduled weekly Cowork run with the Slack MCP and context on his current projects.
  • Claude reports areas of potential misalignment and who he should talk to before shipping.
  • Quality is mixed: he compares it to a coach who is sometimes clearly wrong.
  • Result: per Avasare, the enterprise lead caught cases that would have made teams spin their wheels or duplicate work.

Scheduled Cowork task reviews 20-25 charts each morning

  • solved a problem
  • 20 to 25 charts reviewed daily

A scheduled Cowork task looks at 20 to 25 Hex charts every morning and tells him what is concerning and what is interesting. It covers the long tail of metrics he has no time to check by hand.

Details
  • Built as a scheduled task in the Cowork desktop app with a skill and a list of Hex links.
  • Uses the Chrome extension for some charts and MCP for others.
  • Output is a summary of what needs attention, concerns and insights.
  • He still looks at a few key charts himself and tracks whether false positives and false negatives drop over time.

Claude drafts weekly feedback for his reports and for himself

  • solved a problem

Each week Claude reviews what his direct reports did against team goals, OKRs and meeting transcripts, and suggests feedback. He also asks it to give him feedback as his own manager would.

Details
  • Inputs: team goals and OKRs, discussion transcripts, and Slack via MCP.
  • Output: key takeaways and suggested feedback per person.
  • For upward feedback, Claude uses his manager's public writing plus internal context.
  • He calls the quality hit or miss, with some catches he was very glad to get.

Interview notes

Amol Avasare: How Anthropic Uses Claude to Grow Itself

Amol Avasare, Anthropic's Head of Growth, on Lenny's Podcast (Apr 2026). Anthropic went from about $1B to $19B ARR in 14 months. About 70% of his time goes to "success disasters," where growth breaks other things.

How the team uses agents

  • CASH is Claude running growth experiments: spot an opportunity, build it, check it against the brand bar, analyze results. So far it handles copy and small UI changes, and its hit rate is about that of a junior PM. Humans still approve each step.
  • A weekly Cowork task reads Slack and flags where teams are misaligned or duplicating work.
  • A morning task reviews 20 to 25 dashboards and reports what looks wrong.
  • Claude handles admin: room booking, expenses and email triage.

How the team works

  • Engineers produce 2 to 3 times more, so PMs and designers are now the bottleneck.
  • Two-week rule: for projects of two engineer-weeks or less, the engineer is the PM.
  • 60 to 80% of what they ship has no PRD. Prototypes and Slack threads replace it.
  • Spend 50 to 70% of effort on big bets, not small tweaks, because the product will be far better in two years.

Advice

  • The hard part is activation. Models improve faster than users learn what they can do. Asking users who they are, then routing them, works even though it adds friction.
  • Re-test your tools after each model release.
  • Be willing to give up some metric gains for safety and brand.

Only one long-form 2026 interview was found (his first public interview), so no change of view across interviews can be tracked. All numbers, including the junior-PM win rate, ARR figures and the 2 to 3x engineer output, are his own claims and were not independently verified.
