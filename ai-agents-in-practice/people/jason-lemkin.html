Founder and CEO · SaaStr

Jason Lemkin

Jason Lemkin runs SaaStr, the B2B software community and event business, with about 3 humans and 20+ AI agents. After two salespeople quit in 2025 he replaced most of the go-to-market team with agents for inbound, outbound, lead reactivation, marketing and finance, and reports revenue up with far fewer people.

A general-purpose agent closed a $70K sponsorship

  • moved a metric
  • $70K deal, no human in the loop

Digital Jason, a general agent trained on SaaStr's content and not on sales, closed a $70K sponsorship on its own before SaaStr Annual 2025. That convinced Jason to stop hiring salespeople and push agents instead.

Details
  • Setup: a horizontal agent ('Digital Jason') trained on years of SaaStr content to answer questions.
  • What the agent did: handled an inbound sponsor's questions and emails through to a signed $70K sponsorship.
  • Humans' role: Jason spent about an hour each morning early on reviewing and correcting its answers.
  • Result: proof point for going all in on sales agents after two salespeople quit in May 2025.

Agentforce reactivated leads sales had dropped

  • moved a metric
  • 70% response rate

SaaStr pointed Salesforce Agentforce only at leads the human team decided were not worth their time. Jason reports a 70% response rate.

Details
  • Setup: Agentforce on Salesforce, given a prompt refined over months on their other sales agents.
  • What the agent did: emailed prospects who had contacted sales but never got real follow-up.
  • Humans' role: chose the segment and reused the trained prompt; reps had skipped these leads.
  • Result: 70% response rate per Jason in January; a later SaaStr post cites a 72% open rate on win-back campaigns.

SaaStr hit 140% of its Q1 2025 revenue in Q1 2026 with 1.25 humans in sales, 5 core GTM agents and 20+ agents overall. By September Jason said revenue had doubled over 12 months.

Key learnings

  • Start where you do nothing today: dropped leads, B leads and after-hours inbound. Do not put agents on your A leads first.
  • Train each agent on your best rep's emails and scripts, then correct it for an hour or two a day for 30 days.
  • Give each agent its own segment so agents do not collide, and hyper-segment lists or outbound turns generic.
  • Budget real human time. Jason's estimate grew from 10 to 15 hours a week in January to about 8 hours a day by August, once agents started making decisions instead of tasks.
  • Raise the bar: by May he stopped building agents to match 80% of the best human and started aiming for 120%.
  • Buy most agents, ideally from a vendor with a forward-deployed engineer. His view shifted over the year: SaaStr later built its own outbound and marketing agents on Replit when tools fell short.
  • Consolidate once it works. By July SaaStr was cutting back from 20+ agents toward fewer, broader ones like 10K.
  • Guardrail carefully: too many rules broke an app, and an agent with broad Google Drive access pushed unapproved changes to production, so limit what agents can read and act on.

Interview notes

Jason Lemkin: We Replaced Our Sales Team with 20 AI Agents

Jason Lemkin on Lenny's Podcast (Jan 2026). After two salespeople quit mid-event, SaaStr stopped hiring for sales. About 10 sales staff became 1.2 humans and 20 agents. "The net productivity is about the same," and now it scales.

How to deploy a sales agent

  • Start with support. Their "Digital Jason" clone began by answering event questions and later closed a $70K sponsorship on its own.
  • Buy, don't build. Choose the vendor that will help you most, ideally with a forward-deployed engineer.
  • Train it on your best rep's emails and scripts. Then correct its mistakes for an hour or two a day for 30 days.
  • Give each agent its own segment of customers so agents don't collide.
  • Plan on ongoing oversight. Their part-time Chief AI Officer spends 10 to 15 hours a week reviewing output. "If you just buy one and disappear, you will have zero ROI."

What worked

  • Inbound: a site chatbot qualifies visitors and books meetings around the clock.
  • Reactivation: an agent emailed leads that reps had dropped and got a 70% response rate.
  • Disclosing AI didn't matter. "No one cares."

Predictions

  • Email-cadence SDRs and inbound BDRs will mostly disappear by the end of 2026.
  • AEs keep about 70% of the job for now, but they need to be far more productive.
  • Promote a quantitative person from inside to run the agents.

Try this: once a quarter, use your own product in incognito mode. Fix the worst experience with an agent.
