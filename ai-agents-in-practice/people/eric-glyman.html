Eric Glyman

Eric Glyman co-founded Ramp, a corporate card and finance automation company with 85,000+ business customers. Consolidated learnings · Timeline · Agent stories

Consolidated learnings

  • Let customers write the rules in plain English and have agents apply them, with an audit trail for every decision.
  • Sell the finished work, not the tool: price and pitch around time and money saved, and track both on a shared scoreboard.
  • Let non-engineers ship small changes themselves; measure the time from spotting a problem to fixing it, not headcount cut. Teams still keep about 1 PM, 5 to 8 engineers and 1 designer.
  • Treat token spend as a managed budget line: route routine work to cheaper models and keep frontier models for hard tasks.
  • Give agents identities, cards and spending limits, the way you would onboard a new hire.
  • Invest in moats AI cannot copy cheaply: your own data, network effects and operational detail.
  • View change: in Feb he framed Ramp against banks ("They sell money and we sell time"); by July he called AI labs Ramp's closest peers and token spend a new third pillar of business spending.

Agent stories

Agents review 100,000+ expenses a day

  • solved a problem
  • 100,000+ expenses/day, over 99% accuracy (claimed)

Ramp's expense agent applies each customer's written expense policy to every transaction and decides whether it is in or out of policy. Glyman says it is more accurate than human reviewers.

Details
  • The customer writes its expense policy in plain English.
  • The agent checks receipts, timing and transaction data against that policy.
  • It keeps an audit trail explaining each decision.
  • Managers no longer review routine expenses by hand; a human can still sign off, which supports separation of duties.
  • Glyman says over 100,000 expenses a day are reviewed this way at over 99% accuracy.

Glass prompt grows captured feature requests from 300 to 20,000 a month

  • moved a metric
  • ~300 to ~20,000 feature requests captured per month

An account manager saved a Glass prompt that pulls feature requests out of customer calls. Requests that used to be copied into Salesforce by hand are now captured automatically.

Details
  • Before: feature requests were copied by hand into Salesforce, about 300 a month.
  • An account manager saved a prompt in Glass, Ramp's internal AI workspace, that extracts requests from call recordings.
  • Ramp now captures about 20,000 requests a month.
  • A second saved prompt emails customers when their requested feature ships; over 5,000 such emails have gone out.
  • 323 employees have made 6,500+ edits to what Glass knows.

Source: Ramp blog, Introducing Glass, by Eric Glyman · Oct 13, 2026

Routing Ramp's own AI work cut its model bill 30%

  • moved a metric
  • 30% lower AI bill

Ramp spent $1.5 million on tokens in a single week in May 2026. Moving its own work onto Ramp Router, which picks cheaper models where they are good enough, cut that bill by 30%.

Details
  • Trigger: $1.5M of token spend in one week in May 2026.
  • Ramp Router sends routine tasks to cheaper models and keeps frontier models for hard ones.
  • Glyman says open-weight models often match a frontier model about six months later at a fraction of the cost.
  • Result: Ramp's own bill fell 30% after the switch.

Designers and support staff ship code to production

  • built a feature
  • small UI changes done in minutes

Glyman says designers, marketers and customer support staff now ship small production changes by prompting agents. A change like a button color can be requested, made, verified and validated within minutes.

Details
  • Non-engineers describe the change in natural language.
  • A coding agent makes the change and it is verified before going live.
  • Engineering teams still keep their usual shape: about 1 PM, 5 to 8 engineers and 1 designer.
  • Glyman says the gain is in how fast a seen problem gets fixed, not in cutting headcount.

Interview notes

Eric Glyman: Agents That Review 100,000 Expenses a Day

Ramp CEO Eric Glyman with John Collison on Cheeky Pint (Feb 2026). Ramp's agents review more than 100,000 expenses a day, and he says they're over 99% accurate.

How the expense agent works

  • The customer writes its expense policy in plain English.
  • The agent applies it using the receipt, timing and transaction data.
  • It writes an audit trail for every decision. A human can still sign off, which keeps auditors happy.

How Ramp builds

  • Designers, marketers and support staff ship small changes to production by prompting agents, like changing a button's color.
  • Teams are still about 1 PM, 5 to 8 engineers and 1 designer. Real customers and old systems mean you can't just cut headcount.
  • What matters is how fast you go from seeing a problem to fixing it.

Lessons

  • As software gets cheaper to build, moats matter more: your own data, network effects, operational detail.
  • Sell the finished work, not just the tool. "They sell money and we sell time."
  • Regenerating code from outcomes is promising, but not for systems that need 99.99% uptime.
  • The 99% accuracy figure is Glyman's own claim.

The 100,000 expenses a day, 99% accuracy, 20,000 feature requests a month and 30% bill reduction figures are Ramp's own claims and were not independently verified.
