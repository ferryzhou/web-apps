Fiona Fung

Fiona Fung leads engineering and product for Claude Code and Cowork at Anthropic, after 11 years at Microsoft and senior roles at Meta, including starting Facebook Marketplace. Her team runs agents in the background on schedules, has Claude review code against specs checked into the repo, and rewrote planning and meetings because coding is no longer the bottleneck.

Agent stories

Morning routine turns feedback into PRs

  • solved a problem
  • Manual daily triage replaced by a scheduled routine

She used to read user feedback channels every morning over coffee. After Routines launched, a scheduled routine summarizes the themes and kicks off agents, so she wakes up to PRs to review.

Details
  • Setup: a routine scheduled every morning, pointed at the team's feedback Slack channels.
  • The agent summarizes themes, finds bugs and polish fixes, and spawns agents to draft fixes.
  • Her role: review and land the PRs.
  • In her May talk this was still a manual habit; the slide deck predated the Routines launch.

A Claude session across all repos drives monthly reviews

  • solved a problem

She keeps a remote Claude Code session with access to all team repos, Slack channels and metrics. Each month she reviews what shipped and how it did together with each person, and uses it to find quality hotspots from incidents.

Details
  • Setup: one Claude Code remote session enlisted in every team repo, plus Slack and metrics access.
  • Monthly she screen-shares and asks: what shipped, how did it do, what bugs did we cause.
  • She also asked it to theme recent incidents and suggest the next area of investment.
  • She says a year ago she could not have produced these insights by hand at 8x the code volume.

Claude reviews code against specs in the repo

  • solved a problem
  • Removed human review as the main bottleneck

Human code review was a big bottleneck, and Claude Code reviews did not exist the year before. The team now checks specs and 'what good looks like' into the repo, and Claude reviews each change against them.

Details
  • Claude handles style, lint, bug catching and adding tests.
  • Specs, including a content design check, live in the repo and must be kept in sync with the code.
  • Humans still review legal risk, security-sensitive code and product taste.
  • She describes it as an evolution of test-driven development; her first Claude Code bug fix was done test-first with Claude.

Source: Lenny's Podcast, Jun 2026, ~14:52-16:40 · Jun 2026

Cowork sessions with small business owners led to Claude for Small Business

  • launched something
  • Product launched: Claude for Small Business

She onboarded friends who run small businesses to Cowork. One restaurant owner had Cowork find menus in a messy folder and run a local price comparison. These sessions surfaced onboarding bugs and plugin confusion, and Anthropic later launched Claude for Small Business with bundled plugins.

Details
  • Started from her own use of Cowork for business expenses.
  • Cowork was given access to a restaurant owner's documents folder, found the menus, and produced a market analysis of local pricing.
  • Watching owners go through onboarding found real bugs.
  • Owners kept asking about plugins; the launched product bundles them behind a toggle. She says the launch came from a separate team and she takes no credit.

Every commit Claude-assisted, new hires ship in week one

  • moved a metric
  • No non-Claude-assisted commit seen in 4 months

In her Code w/ Claude talk she said every commit on her team is Claude-assisted by default and new engineers now ship real code in their first week. She tracks ramp time, PR cycle time and Claude-assisted commits, and warns against treating throughput as the goal.

Details
  • Rules: everyone dogfoods Claude Code and Cowork, the team stays flat, anyone may kill an outdated process.
  • A weekly status meeting where people sat on laptops was cancelled after she asked why it existed.
  • PR cycle time can show where CI and build systems struggle with more code.
  • Separately, an Anthropic chart she cites shows engineers shipping about 8x more code per quarter than in 2021-2025.

Source: Code w/ Claude SF, May 2026 (writeup on claude.com, Jun 3 2026) · May 2026

Key learnings

  • Pick your noisiest or most dreaded workflow; ask if it still serves a purpose, then automate it or drop it.
  • Check specs and 'what good looks like' into the repo so Claude can review every change against them; keep humans on legal, security and taste.
  • Turn daily manual habits into scheduled routines that draft work for you, then review the output instead of doing the triage.
  • Plan monthly or just in time, using prototypes and internal users, instead of six-month roadmaps.
  • Give everyone explicit permission to kill processes, and keep the team flat with managers who start as ICs.
  • Hire creative builders with product sense plus deep systems experts for the hard parts.
  • Measure the problem you are solving, not code volume; watch ramp time and PR cycle time for bottlenecks.
  • Plan for the human cost: block focus time for context switching across many agents and add shared maker time, lunches and hackathons so the work does not get lonely.

Interview notes

Fiona Fung: Running the Most AI-Pilled Engineering Team

Fiona Fung, who manages the Claude Code and Cowork teams at Anthropic, on Lenny's Podcast (Jun 2026). "Coding is no longer the bottleneck." She cites an Anthropic chart showing engineers ship about 8 times more code per quarter than in 2025.

How her team works

  • Agents run in the background on schedules. A morning routine summarizes user feedback channels and drafts PRs for her to review.
  • Once a month, a Claude session with access to all team repos, Slack and metrics reviews what each person shipped. She uses it to start one-on-one conversations.
  • Specs in the repo define what "good" looks like, and Claude reviews code against them. "Claude is very good when you give it a framework to validate against."
  • PMs and designers check in code.
  • Monthly planning replaced six-month roadmaps. Teams have "explicit permission to kill processes that no longer serve us."
  • Bugs are graded "bad" (lost work, unrecoverable) or "sad" (annoying but recoverable).

Lessons and open problems

  • Running about 20 agents at once means constant context switching. She blocks focus time to catch up.
  • How far to trust fully automated review is still open.
  • Training junior engineers who rarely read code is a real concern.
  • Hire two profiles: creative builders with product sense, and deep systems experts.
  • "High agency is also high accountability."

The 8x code figure, the four months without a non-assisted commit and the faster onboarding are her or Anthropic's own claims, not independently verified. The Code w/ Claude talk details come from Anthropic's own writeup rather than a transcript, and the January Peterman Pod is a career interview with little on agents; her view moved from manual morning triage (May) to fully scheduled routines plus new worries about context switching and loneliness (June).
