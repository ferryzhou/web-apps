Agent stories

River coauthors one in eight merged pull requests

  • moved a metric
  • 1 in 8 merged PRs; 3,536 PRs in 30 days

River is Shopify's Slack-native agent, built on its Aquifer agent platform. Within a couple of months of launch it coauthored about one in eight merged pull requests across Shopify.

Details
  • Staff @-mention River in public Slack channels; it reads code, runs tests, queries the warehouse, reads production traces and opens PRs.
  • In one 30-day window it ran 59,918 sessions across 5,170 channels with over 7,000 people involved.
  • Median session was 19 minutes and 50 tool calls.
  • Humans review and merge every PR and write the skills and AGENTS.md files River relies on.

Source: Shopify Engineering blog, Under the River, May 2026 · May 28, 2026

River's merge rate doubles by working in public

  • moved a metric
  • Merge rate 36% to 77% in two months

Lütke says River's pull request merge rate rose from 36% to 77% over two months. He credits people watching River in public channels, seeing where it got stuck and writing down what it should know, not a new model.

Details
  • River refuses direct messages, so every session is a searchable transcript.
  • He calls this a 'Lehrwerkstatt', a teaching workshop where people learn by watching.
  • That week River opened 1,870 PRs in the main monorepo, and about one in eight merged PRs was River-authored and staff-reviewed.
  • 5,938 employees used River in the prior 30 days.

Source: Tobi Lütke on X, May 2026 (via aggregator) · May 9, 2026

River usage more than triples in six months

  • moved a metric
  • 35,418 to 121,804 Slack threads a month

Lütke posted that River handled 121,804 Slack threads in September 2026, up from 35,418 in March. The number of employees appearing in River threads rose from 4,147 to 6,273.

Details
  • He titled the chart 'River floods Shopify'.
  • Links between people sharing the same River threads grew from 61,680 to 171,853.
  • He posted it in response to Anthropic's study on how employment is changing there.
  • It shows adoption, not code quality or business impact.

Source: Tobi Lütke on X, Oct 2026 · Oct 19, 2026

Voice wish becomes an open-source screenshot tool

  • built a feature
  • Built in hours; six outside PRs overnight

On his Linux (Omarchy) setup, Lütke voice-messaged a request for a screenshot annotation tool and an agent built it in hours. He open-sourced it and six outside pull requests arrived overnight.

Details
  • He described the request as a voice message to his agent.
  • The tool is omasnap, a Wayland screenshot and annotation editor for Omarchy and Hyprland.
  • The repo has an AGENTS.md file and has grown to about 575 stars and 300+ commits.
  • He used it to illustrate software that 'fulfills wishes'.

Source: The Knowledge Project, Sep 2026 · Sep 15, 2026

An AI council prepares his hard decisions

  • solved a problem
  • About $15 to $20 in tokens per decision

For a hard decision, Lütke's AI chief of staff spins up sub-agents across several models to study it from data, research, business and engineering angles, then synthesizes. He says a run costs about $15 to $20 in tokens versus weeks of human deliberation.

Details
  • Sub-agents run on different models to get different views.
  • The chief-of-staff agent merges the results into one brief.
  • He makes the call himself; the goal is 'the right environment for judgment'.
  • He compares it to a Bloomberg terminal for decisions.

Source: The Knowledge Project, Sep 2026 · Sep 15, 2026

Key learnings

  • Make the company agent work only in public channels, so people learn from each other's sessions and fixes spread.
  • Improve agents by mining their transcripts and writing skills and AGENTS.md files, not by waiting for a better model.
  • Give the agent a name and personality and let it push back. Bland assistants are less useful.
  • Use agents to prepare decisions, such as a multi-model council, but keep a named human accountable for the call.
  • Read before you forward. Guard against 'slop grenades' of unreviewed AI output.
  • Make AI use an explicit expectation and tell people honestly how much it changes output, but avoid token leaderboards, which he says caused bad behavior.
  • Invest in the plumbing first. Shopify's monorepo and Nix work made River possible, and every change for agents also helped humans.
  • His view shifted in 2026: in May he said he now thinks senior engineers beat juniors at steering agents, and he dropped the six-week cycle as too slow for AI-era work.

Interview notes

Tobi Lütke: How Shopify Works with AI Agents

Shopify CEO Tobi Lütke with Shane Parrish on The Knowledge Project (Sep 2026). His main point: as AI gets more capable, taste, judgment and responsibility become the scarce human skills. Full notes: my earlier post.

How Shopify uses agents

  • Few engineers write code by hand. They each coordinate 10 to 50 agents.
  • River is Shopify's AI colleague. It has a name, memory for each Slack channel, codebase access, and permission to push back on leadership.
  • About half of Shopify's pull requests start from a conversation with River.
  • River works only in open channels, so people learn by watching each other use it.
  • Every night River reviews its own conversations and rewrites its skill files.

How Tobi uses agents

  • For a hard decision he runs an "AI council": sub-agents across several models look at it from different angles, then he synthesizes. It costs about $15 to $20 in tokens.
  • The AI informs the decision. He still makes it.

Lessons

  • Watch out for "slop grenades," AI output sent on without being read. Review and compression are the real work now.
  • Give agents a personality. Bland assistants are less useful.
  • Prune and rebuild instead of adding layers. Hold "refounding" events.
  • Machines can't be held accountable, so people must own the decisions.

River figures come from Shopify's blog and Lütke's own posts and are not independently audited. He told The Knowledge Project that about half of PRs start from a River conversation, while Shopify's blog says one in eight merged PRs is River-coauthored; the May X post was read through an aggregator, and that omasnap was agent-built rests on his own account.
