Geoff Charles

Geoff Charles is Ramp's Chief Product Officer and runs what he calls an AI software factory for the product team. Ramp's internal agents now find customer problems, write specs, build about 75% of pull requests, review code and test it, by his own account.

Latest view (Sep 30, 2026): As of September 2026 he says coding is largely solved at Ramp, so the bottleneck is product work, and PMs should build agents that find, define, review, test and coordinate the work.

Consolidated learnings

  • Find the bottleneck: once agents write most code, invest in discovery, specs, review and QA.
  • Give engineers evidence, requirements a coding agent can act on, and a prototype, not just a long spec.
  • Build agents over raw customer data (calls, tickets, sessions) instead of reading feedback by hand, and keep every insight linked to its source.
  • Run coding agents in cloud sandboxes with real tests and previews, so anyone, including non-engineers, can ship safely.
  • Make the company legible to agents: keep decisions and status in Notion, Slack and Linear so agents can answer questions.
  • Drive adoption on purpose: unlimited tokens, a proficiency ladder, leaderboards, hackathons and AI skills in hiring.
  • View change: in March AI wrote about 50% of code and PMs were told to become builders or GMs; by September agents built 75% of PRs and he split PM work into factory builders, tastemakers and GMs.

Agent stories

Customer feedback agent does 8 days of analysis in minutes

  • solved a problem
  • 90 days of feedback analyzed in ~8 minutes vs ~8 human days

Ramp's voice-of-customer agent searched 90 days of support tickets and chats and surfaced the main themes with links to sources. Charles says a human analyst would have needed about eight days.

Details
  • Started as a Slack "hate channel" of negative customer quotes that grew unmanageable.
  • Rebuilt with ETL pipelines, vector search and clustering over Gong, Zendesk and LogRocket data.
  • Returns themes with links back to the original tickets and calls.
  • Now available company-wide, including a daily audio digest of customer complaints.

Inspect coding agent builds 75% of Ramp's pull requests

  • moved a metric
  • ~30% (Jan) to ~75% of merged PRs; 1M+ sessions

Inspect is Ramp's in-house background coding agent, started from Slack or the web, running in a cloud sandbox. Its share of merged PRs grew from about 30% to about 75% during 2026.

Details
  • Each session gets a full dev environment in a Modal sandbox and starts in seconds; built on the open-source OpenCode agent.
  • It runs tests, checks telemetry and feature flags, and shows screenshots and live previews of frontend changes.
  • PRs open under the requesting user's GitHub account, so they cannot approve their own change.
  • Share of merged PRs: ~30% early 2026 per Ramp's blog, ~75% by May per The Pragmatic Engineer; 1M sessions passed in July.
  • About 1,000 PRs a month now come from non-engineers; a team of about 5 runs it.

Inspect builds a live bills metrics feature in about five minutes

  • built a feature
  • full-stack feature in ~5 minutes

On air, Charles showed Inspect building a bills metrics view (overdue, upcoming, 0 to 30 days, 30 to 90 days, total outstanding) on Ramp's real front end and back end in about five minutes.

Details
  • Charles gave a plain-language prompt for the feature.
  • Inspect wrote the frontend and backend code and opened a PR with a preview.
  • A human reviews the PR before merge; a double-digit share of PRs is auto-approved.
  • He says this is why roughly half of Ramp's code was AI-written by March.

Review and QA agents handle 93% of PRs and caught 425 bugs in 30 days

  • moved a metric
  • 93% of PRs auto-handled; 425 bugs caught in 30 days

Review Buddy reviews code for quality and security and routes PRs to the right human. Testo, a browser QA agent, tests the product in about 100 combinations built from production data.

Details
  • Review Buddy knows the codebase and sees the prompts that produced the code.
  • It handles 93% of PRs on its own and routes the rest to the right reviewer.
  • Review Buddy was built by one engineer in about a week, per The Pragmatic Engineer.
  • Testo returns bugs and design feedback; it caught 425 bugs in the 30 days before the talk.

60% of reported UX issues fixed within 24 hours

  • moved a metric
  • 60% of UX issues fixed within 24 hours

Ramp built a loop that takes small UX issues flagged by customers, sales, CX or staff and fixes them with one human approval. Charles says 60% are fixed within a day.

Details
  • An agent routes each issue, matches it to the backlog, dedupes and ranks it.
  • It writes a plan and asks for a single approval in Slack.
  • A coding agent makes the fix, runs tests and CI, and updates docs.
  • A separate agent answers 85% of questions sent to PMs from Notion, Slack and Linear.

Company-wide rollout gets 99.5% of staff using AI

  • moved a metric
  • 99.5% of staff active; 1,500+ apps in 6 weeks

Charles described how Ramp pushed AI use across every team. He reports 99.5% of staff active on AI tools and 1,500+ internal apps built in six weeks by 800+ people.

Details
  • Unlimited tokens, no approval workflows, tools pre-connected in Glass.
  • An L0 to L3 proficiency ladder, usage leaderboards, and AI skills as a hiring requirement.
  • A 700-person internal hackathon coached by 100 engineers and PMs; a skills marketplace (Dojo) with 350+ skills.
  • Result: 84% use coding agents weekly and non-engineers write 12% of human-started PRs.

Interview notes

Geoff Charles: Inside Ramp, Where AI Agents Run Everything

Ramp CPO Geoff Charles on Peter Yang's Behind the Craft (Mar 2026). About 50% of Ramp's production code is AI-written, up from about 30% in December, and the target is 80%. "My job is to automate my job."

Agents Ramp built

  • Voice of the customer: searches tickets, chats, sales notes and call recordings. It analyzed 90 days of feedback in about 8 minutes, work he says would take a person 8 days.
  • Spec writer: a Claude Code skill questions the PM on goals and trade-offs and pushes back on weak answers. It runs 6 to 10 research agents over competitors, sales calls, tickets and the codebase, then writes a 2-minute spec.
  • Inspect: an internal coding agent that builds full-stack features from a prompt and opens PRs.
  • Ramp Research: anyone can ask data questions and get a full report back.
  • Releases bot: writes help articles and impact reports for each launch.

How they drive adoption

  • An L0 to L3 AI proficiency ladder, with usage tracked company-wide.
  • No budget limits on AI tools.
  • Candidates build and demo something in the interview.
  • Staged rollout: dogfood, alpha, then about 10% of customers, then everyone.

Lessons

  • "If you're not using Claude Code this year, no matter what your role is, you're probably underperforming."
  • AI, not engineers, now reads the spec. Keep it short and precise.
  • "Management is probably dead." Be the best builder you can.

All figures (75% of PRs, 93% auto-review, 425 bugs, 60% UX fixes, 99.5% adoption) are self-reported by Charles or Ramp; Lenny's talk numbers come from third-party write-ups (BuildBetter, Dealroom), not a transcript. Inspect's PR share differs by source and date (about 30%, "over half", 60%, 75%), so treat it as a rising estimate.
