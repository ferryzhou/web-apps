Bret Taylor

Bret Taylor co-founded Sierra, which builds customer-facing AI agents for large companies, and chairs the OpenAI board. In 2026 he described agents that resolve 70 to 90% of support cases for customers like SoFi, Rocket Mortgage and Ramp, kept reliable by simulations, AI monitors and outcome-based pricing.

Agent stories

SoFi raises chat NPS by 33 points

  • moved a metric
  • +33 NPS, 61% containment

SoFi replaced a basic chat tool with a Sierra agent that answers banking, card, investing and loan questions. Taylor cites the 33-point NPS gain on Cheeky Pint.

Details
  • SoFi has over 13.7 million members and peaks around paydays and tax season.
  • The agent calls SoFi's internal APIs under compliance guardrails.
  • Staff handle the complex cases and review the agent's reasoning traces.
  • Three months after launch: 61% containment, over 50,000 conversations a week, chat NPS up 33 points.

Rocket's AI assistant triples close rates

  • moved a metric
  • 3x close rate; 4x conversion with AI plus banker

Rocket Mortgage runs a Sierra agent across chat and voice that takes buyers from home search on Redfin to preapproval and servicing. Clients who start with the assistant close at three times the rate of those who don't.

Details
  • The agent answers questions, pulls credit, presents rates and helps with payments and loan status.
  • Bankers stay in the loop through a handoff from the agent.
  • It grew from a proof of concept to over 400,000 chat conversations and over one million outbound dials a month.
  • Clients using both AI chat and a banker convert at 4x for refinance and purchase.

Source: Big Technology Podcast, Jan 2026, ~34:02; Cheeky Pint, Mar 2026, ~23:33; Sierra Rocket customer story · Jan 2026

Retailer turns AI savings into 2 to 3x more conversations

  • solved a problem
  • 2-3x customer conversations at flat cost

An unnamed retailer kept support costs roughly flat after deploying an agent, because conversation volume rose 2 to 3 times. Its CEO said the company was now actually listening to customers.

Details
  • AI cases cost cents, versus $10 to $20 for a human-handled case.
  • Customers contacted the brand far more once support was instant.
  • Human agents got the harder cases; handle time rose but agent satisfaction rose too.
  • Taylor's point: the gain showed up as more customer contact, not lower cost.

Cigna agent live in under two months

  • launched something
  • Live in under 2 months

Sierra launched an agent for Cigna that helps members understand benefits and process claims. Taylor says it went live in less than two months.

Details
  • Use case: benefits questions and claims for health insurance members.
  • Before each release, customers run hundreds or thousands of simulated conversations, like a regression test suite.
  • AI monitors flag hallucinations or frustrated customers, and humans review only the flagged cases.
  • Taylor also describes payer and provider agents now talking to each other on the phone.

Source: Big Technology Podcast, Jan 2026, ~33:23 · Jan 2026

Key learnings

  • Start with one channel and a few narrow use cases, then expand; automate one contract type and route exceptions to people.
  • Give each process an owner and a measurable goal; treat the process, not the person, as the unit to automate.
  • Test agents like software: run hundreds or thousands of simulated conversations before every release.
  • Use AI monitors and supervisor models to check the agent's work, and have humans review only the flagged cases.
  • Let the agent query messy, duplicate systems and reason over conflicts instead of waiting for a data cleanup project.
  • Price and measure on outcomes such as resolved cases, not token usage.
  • Expect your own scaffolding to be thrown away as models improve, and buy specialized agents rather than building generic ones.
  • His view was consistent across Jan to Mar 2026; the main shift was emphasis, from agents replacing dashboards (Jan) to generic agent builders commoditizing (Feb) to process ownership as the leadership task (Mar).

Interview notes

Bret Taylor: What Sierra Learned Deploying AI Agents

Sierra CEO Bret Taylor with John Collison on Cheeky Pint (Mar 2026). Sierra builds customer-service agents for large companies. It reached about $100M ARR in seven quarters. Its customers automate 70 to 90% of support cases.

What he's seen in deployments

  • Start with one channel and a few use cases, then add more.
  • A human-handled case costs $10 to $20. An AI case costs cents, so companies can afford good service for every customer.
  • Second-order effects show up. Human agents get only the hard cases and say they're more satisfied. One retailer's costs stayed flat, but conversations grew 2 to 3 times.
  • Let the agent reason across messy systems instead of waiting on a big IT cleanup.
  • Customers set goals and guardrails. A supervisor model checks the agent's work.
  • Expect to throw your own code away when models catch up.

Pricing

  • Charge for outcomes, not usage: a fee per resolved case, nothing when a case goes to a human. "Token usage isn't value."

Lessons for leaders

  • "The atomic unit of productivity in AI is a process, not a person." Give each process an owner and a goal, like cutting supplier onboarding from 17 days to one.
  • Narrow the problem. Don't build an AI for every contract. Automate one contract type and send the exceptions to legal.
  • Handing everyone Copilot doesn't make you an AI company.
