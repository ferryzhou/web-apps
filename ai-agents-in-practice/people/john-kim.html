Consolidated learnings

  • Run internal AI adoption like a product, with a dedicated team reporting to the CEO and a weekly task force that includes engineering and security.
  • Let anyone post the work they want automated, and reward whoever ships it, human or agent.
  • Give non-engineers a pre-approved template with login, database, security and hosting, because they will build anyway.
  • Share agent skills in one marketplace so teams do not rebuild the same thing.
  • Measure token use openly, but use it to coach low users, not to rate people.
  • Have leaders use the tools heavily and visibly, and start with curious champions who get the spotlight.
  • In customer-facing agents, let AI execute and keep humans on judgment and approvals.

Marketing built a swag store with payments, no engineers

  • launched something
  • Built in days without engineering

Sendbird's marketing team built and launched a branded swag store with Stripe payments using AI coding tools. Kim says engineering did not help, and it would otherwise have cost two sprints on the product roadmap.

Details
  • Setup: marketers used AI coding tools on the company's pre-approved app template.
  • They built the store end to end, including Stripe checkout and an Easter egg.
  • Engineering was not involved, so the idea did not compete with the product roadmap.
  • Kim presents it as an example of giving creative non-engineers the power to ship to customers.

Automators: a quest board for internal automations

  • built a feature
  • Each quest shows risk and weeks saved

Sendbird built an internal platform where anyone can post a quest for an automation they need. Engineers, other staff or AI can pick it up; Kim says AI can now read the spec, write a PRD and start coding.

Details
  • A finance or recruiting person posts the workflow they want automated.
  • Each quest lists its risk level, weeks saved and who benefits.
  • Builders earn points redeemable for gift cards, tea with an executive or a demo slot at standup.
  • People use spare time between core work to take quests that sit outside the main code repository.

Token leaderboard with leaders at the top

  • moved a metric
  • Kim averages 30-50M tokens a day; top tier is over 100M

Sendbird tracks AI token use per person and team in five tiers, from Beginner to 'AI God' at over 100 million tokens a day. Kim says the biggest users are the CTO and his co-founder, which signals to everyone how work is done.

Details
  • Managers see which tier their team is in and tailor training to it.
  • Kim says it is for learning and coaching, not performance reviews, and compares it to the old mistake of measuring lines of code.
  • He reports a 30-day average of 30-50 million tokens a day, with a peak near 200 million.
  • He splits his own use about 80% Claude Code and 20% Codex.

Agent QA tooling cut fix time from a week to 1-2 days

  • moved a metric
  • Fix and redeploy: about a week to 1-2 days; weekly PRs about doubled

A Sendbird AI/ML engineer used Claude Code to replace per-engineer notebooks with shared tooling to debug and regression-test customers' Delight.ai agents. Fixing and redeploying a production agent issue went from about a week to one or two days.

Details
  • Before: each engineer kept a personal Python notebook to generate and label test conversations.
  • A conversation debugger compares expected and actual agent behavior, and Opus suggests what to fix.
  • A regression tool generates test conversations at scale; every customer deployment now runs through it.
  • Weekly PRs created rose from about 700 to about 1,600 and merged PRs from about 600 to about 1,300 between November and May.

Interview notes

John Kim: Turning a Whole Company into AI Builders

Sendbird CEO John Kim with Claire Vo on How I AI (May 2026). His playbook for getting every employee building with agents, not just engineers.

What they built

  • Quests: anyone posts a task they want automated, like chasing receivables or sourcing candidates. Engineers, other employees or AI agents claim it. Each quest shows its risk and the weeks it saves.
  • Rewards: points that buy gift cards, tea with an executive, or a demo slot at the weekly standup.
  • Safe template: a pre-approved app template with login, database, security review and hosting built in, so non-engineers can ship safely.
  • Skills marketplace: teams share agent skills so nobody builds the same thing twice.
  • Token leaderboard: five tiers, from Beginner (under 1M tokens a day) to "AI God" (over 100M). It's for coaching, not performance reviews. He meets one-on-one with low users to find what's blocking them.

Results

  • Marketing built a swag store with payments in a day or two, with no engineering help.
  • The biggest token users are the CTO and his co-founder. He uses 30 to 50M tokens a day himself.
  • A smooth usage curve means agents are working around the clock. Dips mean people are on vacation.

Lessons

  • Treat internal tooling as a product, not a program.
  • Start with your curious champions.
  • Hire for curiosity, agency and energy over years of experience.
  • Put one person in charge of internal AI, with a weekly task force including security.
